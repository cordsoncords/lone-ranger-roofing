A roof warranty feels solid right up until something drills forty mounting brackets into it. That is what a solar installation is, from the roof's point of view, and most Temple homeowners never think to ask their solar company how those penetrations interact with the warranty they already have on their shingles.

We have walked more than a few roofs in Temple where solar went up cleanly from an electrical standpoint but left the roof warranty in a gray area nobody flagged ahead of time. The panels work fine. The paperwork underneath them is the part that gets overlooked, and it is worth understanding before an installer shows up with a truck full of racking.

## How Solar Panels Interact With a Roof Warranty

Most manufacturer roof warranties cover material defects, and some cover workmanship, but nearly all of them include language about unauthorized penetrations or modifications to the roofing system. A solar mount is a penetration. Whether that penetration is covered under the original warranty terms depends on who installs it, how it is flashed, and whether the installer followed the manufacturer's specifications for working around existing roofing material.

This is not a reason to avoid solar. It is a reason to ask the right questions before installation day, specifically who is responsible if a leak shows up at a mounting point two years from now, and whether that responsibility falls on the solar company, the roofing manufacturer, or the homeowner alone.

## What Voids a Manufacturer's Warranty

The fastest way to void a shingle manufacturer's warranty during a solar install is improper flashing around the mounting brackets, or using a mounting method the manufacturer has not approved for that specific roofing product. Some manufacturers publish a list of approved solar mounting systems and installation methods. Solar companies do not always check that list, since their focus is the electrical system rather than the roofing warranty sitting underneath it.

A [roof replacement](/residential-roofing/roof-replacement) done at the same time as solar removes this risk almost entirely, since the roofing contractor and the solar installer can coordinate on flashing details from the start rather than the solar crew working around an existing warranty they were never told about.

### Labor Warranties Matter as Much as Material Ones

A material warranty covers the shingles themselves. It does not always cover the labor to fix a leak that develops around a solar mount. We warranty our labor the same way we warranty the materials, because that is usually where the real problems start on a roof with solar hardware attached to it. A leak traced back to poor flashing work is a labor issue, not a shingle defect, and homeowners are often surprised to learn their material warranty does not touch it.

## Why Installation Method Matters as Much as the Panels

Two solar companies can install the same panels on the same roof and leave very different warranty situations behind. A crew that flashes each mounting point correctly, seals penetrations to manufacturer spec, and documents the work leaves a roof in a position where both the roofing warranty and the solar warranty hold up if something goes wrong. A crew focused on connecting panels and moving to the next job can leave gaps that surface as leaks well after the installation crew is gone.

A [roof inspection](/residential-roofing/roof-inspection) after a solar install, even one done a year or two later, can catch flashing issues before they turn into interior water damage. This is a reasonable step for any Temple homeowner who had solar installed without a roofing contractor directly involved in the mounting work.

## FAQ

**Will my roof warranty automatically get voided by adding solar?**
Not automatically, but it depends on the mounting method and whether the installer followed the manufacturer's approved process for penetrations. Checking the warranty terms before installation protects the coverage.

**Who is responsible if a leak develops at a solar mounting point?**
This depends on the specific warranty language and who performed the work. Getting this answer in writing before installation avoids a dispute over responsibility later.

**Does replacing my roof before solar installation help protect the warranty?**
Yes. A new roof installed with solar mounting planned from the start typically keeps both the roofing and solar warranties intact, since there is no existing warranty being modified after the fact.

**Can a roofing company inspect solar mounting work after it's already installed?**
Yes, and it is worth doing if a roofing contractor was not part of the original solar installation. An inspection can catch flashing problems before they become a leak.
