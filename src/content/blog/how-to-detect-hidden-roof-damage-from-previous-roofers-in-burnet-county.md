Burnet County has been through enough hail seasons in the last decade that most roofs out here have been replaced at least once. A good number of those jobs were done in the rush after a storm, by crews that set up a yard sign, knocked on doors for a month, and moved on to the next county. Some did fine work. Others left problems that won't show up until a hard rain or the next wind event.

The hard part for a homeowner is that this kind of damage hides. From the driveway in Burnet or out toward Marble Falls, the roof looks like a roof. The shortcuts are underneath: flashing that got reused instead of replaced, nails driven through the wrong part of the shingle, underlayment that wasn't lapped, soft decking that was covered over instead of swapped out. Here's how we find it.

## What Hidden Roof Damage From a Rushed Job Looks Like

### Reused or face-nailed flashing

The metal around chimneys, walls, and valleys is supposed to be replaced when a roof is torn off. Pulling old flashing, cleaning it up, and bending it back into place is faster and cheaper, and it's a common corner to cut. Reused counterflashing that's been face-nailed and caulked to the brick rather than set into the mortar will start letting water into the wall within a few Burnet winters.

### Nailing problems you can't see

Shingle manufacturers specify a narrow nailing zone and a set number of nails per shingle. A crew working too fast overdrives nails, underdrives them so the heads sit proud, or places them above the strip entirely. None of that is visible once the next course is down, but it's why shingles start blowing off a three-year-old roof in a storm that shouldn't have touched it.

### Decking that was covered, not corrected

If the old roof leaked, the plywood underneath may be soft, delaminated, or stained with mold. A proper tear-off replaces those sheets. A rushed one lays new shingles over questionable decking because nobody's going to see it. You feel it later as spongy spots underfoot and fasteners that won't hold.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How can I tell if my previous roofer did a bad job?
Often you can't from the ground. The signs are under the shingles and in the attic: flashing stuck on with caulk, nail heads sitting high, no underlayment lap at the eaves, and moisture or staining on the decking. An inspection that gets on the roof and into the attic is the way to know.

### My roof is a few years old. Could it already have hidden damage?
Yes. Installation shortcuts don't wait for the roof to age. Overdriven nails, missing flashing, and covered-over decking cause failures on roofs that are three or four years old, usually during the first significant storm.

### Is hidden damage from a past roofer covered by warranty?
It depends on who installed it and whether that company still stands behind its work. Many storm-chasing crews are gone or unreachable. A local contractor's workmanship warranty is worth something here, but the contractor has to still be around to honor it.

### Does insurance cover damage caused by poor installation?
Generally no. Insurance covers sudden storm events, not workmanship. If a later storm caused damage that a proper installation would have withstood, a claim may still apply to that event.
