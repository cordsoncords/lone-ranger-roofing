Solar quotes tend to move fast in Burnet once a homeowner shows interest, with a proposal and an installation date often landing within a couple of weeks. What rarely gets slowed down enough to matter is a real look at the roof those panels are about to sit on. Most solar sales reps are not roofers, and a quick photo from a drone is not the same thing as someone walking the roof and checking what is actually underneath the shingles.

We get called into these conversations after the fact more often than before it, usually because a homeowner wants a second opinion once they realize their solar company never mentioned the roof's condition at all. Here is what actually determines whether a Burnet roof is solar-ready, and what to check before signing anything.

## Signs Your Burnet Roof Can Handle Solar

The first thing that matters is age. A roof under ten years old with no history of leaks or major storm damage is generally a solid candidate for solar installation. Burnet sits in hail country, and roofs here take repeated hits from storms rolling through the Hill Country each spring, so a roof's storm history matters as much as its calendar age.

The second thing is the deck underneath the shingles. Soft spots, sagging, or visible daylight in the attic around roof penetrations are signs the deck has already taken on moisture, and mounting solar hardware into a compromised deck is asking for trouble. A sound deck holds the mounting brackets securely and keeps water where it belongs, outside the house.

The third is flashing condition around existing penetrations like vents and chimneys. If those are already showing wear, adding more penetration points for solar mounts without addressing the existing ones first is a mistake that shows up as a leak within a year or two.

## What a Roof Check Involves Before Solar Goes Up

A proper [roof inspection](/residential-roofing/roof-inspection) ahead of a solar quote covers the deck, the shingle condition, the flashing, and the overall structure, going well beyond a visual pass from the driveway. We check for granule loss, cracked or lifted shingles, and any soft spots by walking the roof directly rather than relying on satellite imagery, which misses the details that matter most for a mounting job.

This check also gives a homeowner a clear number: how many years of life are left in the current roof. That number should drive the decision on whether solar goes on now or waits until after a [roof replacement](/residential-roofing/roof-replacement). Installing panels on a roof with three or four years left means paying to remove and reinstall them sooner than most homeowners expect.

## When It Makes Sense to Replace First

If a roof inspection turns up a deck in good shape and a shingle life of ten years or more, solar can typically move forward on the existing roof without complication. If the inspection turns up a roof closer to the end of its service life, replacing it before solar goes up saves money in the long run, since the mounting hardware then goes onto a roof that will outlast the solar system's warranty period rather than needing to come off partway through it.

Burnet homeowners who plan the replacement and the solar installation together often save on labor costs too, since some solar installers offer better terms when working with a fresh roof deck that makes their mounting job faster and cleaner.

## FAQ

**How do I know if my Burnet roof needs a full check before solar?**
If your roof is more than eight years old, has been through recent hail activity, or you have never had it professionally inspected, a check before signing a solar contract is worth the time it takes.

**Can a solar company tell me if my roof is ready?**
Some will give a general opinion, but most solar companies are not roofing contractors and will not catch deck or flashing issues that a roofing-specific inspection would find.

**What happens if solar goes up on a roof that needed repairs first?**
Water can find its way in around the mounting points, and diagnosing the leak becomes harder with panels already installed. Repairs also take longer and cost more once panels need to be removed first.

**Does a solar-ready roof need any special materials?**
Not necessarily. Most standard asphalt shingle and metal roofs can support solar mounting hardware, as long as the deck and flashing are in good condition before installation.
