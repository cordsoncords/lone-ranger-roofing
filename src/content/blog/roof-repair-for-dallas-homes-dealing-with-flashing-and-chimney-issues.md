Drive through Lakewood, the M Streets, or Lake Highlands and you'll see a lot of Dallas homes built in an era when a brick chimney was standard on every house. Those chimneys are still up there decades later, and they are the number one reason we get roof repair calls in those neighborhoods. The shingles around them can look fine from the street while water is running down the framing inside.

Flashing is the other half of the story. It's the metal that seals the seams where your roof meets a wall, a chimney, a valley, or a vent. When a Dallas roof leaks, the flashing or the chimney is the culprit far more often than a bad shingle. Hail cracks it, wind lifts it, and forty years of Texas temperature swings work the metal loose from the masonry.

We've traced enough leaks back to these two spots to know where to look before we're off the ladder. Here's how flashing and chimney problems show up on Dallas roofs, what a real repair involves, and when the damage has gone far enough that a patch won't hold.

## Why Flashing and Chimney Issues Drive Most Roof Repair Calls in Dallas

A roof is a system of surfaces and the connections between them. The flat runs of shingle shed water well for 20 years or more. The trouble is at every place the roof plane gets interrupted, because that's where water has to be redirected instead of running straight off.

Dallas puts extra stress on those connection points. Spring hail hits flashing metal at an angle and dents or splits it. Straight-line winds and the occasional tornado-warned storm lift shingles and the flashing tucked under them. Then summer bakes the roof to 150 degrees and winter drops it below freezing, and that swing expands and contracts the metal against the brick until the seal fails. The shingle field ages slowly and predictably. The transitions fail early.

Storm-chasing crews that roll into Dallas after a hailstorm tend to skip this work. There's no full roof to sell in a flashing repair, so they quote a replacement or they do a fast caulk-over that looks fine on the invoice and leaks again by the next season. Our [roof repair](/residential-roofing/roof-repair) work in Dallas is built around getting these details right, because a roof that leaks at the chimney doesn't need to be thrown away.

## How Roof Flashing Fails on Dallas Homes

### Step flashing and counterflashing

Where your roof meets a vertical wall or the side of a chimney, two layers of metal do the work. Step flashing is a series of L-shaped pieces woven into each course of shingles. Counterflashing sits above it, embedded into the mortar joints of the brick, covering the top edge so water can't get behind it.

On older Dallas homes we frequently find counterflashing that was face-nailed to the brick and sealed with caulk instead of being cut into the mortar. That caulk has a lifespan of a few years in this climate. Once it shrinks and cracks, water runs straight behind the step flashing and into the wall. The fix is to grind a proper reglet into the mortar and set new counterflashing into it, which lasts decades instead of seasons.

### Valley and headwall flashing

Valleys carry the most concentrated water flow on any roof, sometimes runoff from two large slopes funneled into one channel. Valley flashing that's undersized, nailed too close to the center, or corroded from years of debris sitting on it will eventually let water under the shingles on either side.

Headwall flashing, where a lower roof section meets a taller wall, fails in a similar way. If the piece is too short, or the siding and brick above it weren't lapped over it correctly, wind-driven Dallas rain gets in at that seam. These are targeted repairs when caught early and a source of rotted decking when they're not.

## The Chimney Is the Hardest-Working Penetration on Your Roof

### The chimney cricket a lot of Dallas roofs are missing

Any chimney wider than about 30 inches on the up-slope side needs a cricket, which is a small peaked structure built behind it that splits water around the chimney instead of letting it pond against the back wall. A surprising number of Dallas homes were built without one, or lost it in a past re-roof.

Without a cricket, leaves and debris pile up behind the chimney, water sits there through every rain, and the flashing in that spot is under constant load. It's one of the first places we check on a chimney leak call, and adding a properly built cricket during a repair often solves a problem the homeowner has been chasing for years.

### Brick, crown, and mortar problems that read as roof leaks

Not every "roof leak" near a chimney is the roof. The concrete crown on top can crack and let water into the masonry. Mortar joints erode and go porous. A missing chimney cap lets rain straight down the flue. Water from any of these travels down through the brick and shows up as a stain on the ceiling that looks exactly like a flashing failure.

Part of a real chimney repair is figuring out which problem you actually have. We check the crown, the cap, and the mortar along with the flashing, because sealing perfect new flashing under a cracked crown means you'll be back on the phone after the next storm. When the damage is widespread, [storm damage repair](/residential-roofing/storm-damage-repair) on the roof may need to happen alongside masonry work.

## What a Proper Flashing and Chimney Roof Repair Looks Like

A repair done right starts with pulling the shingles back around the affected area rather than working over the top of them. New step flashing gets woven into the courses. Counterflashing gets cut into the mortar, not glued to the face. The chimney gets a cricket if it needs one, and the crown and cap get addressed if they're part of the problem.

Most flashing and chimney repairs in Dallas are a few hours of work once the crew is set up, and we finish them in a single visit so there's no open roof left overnight. ## When Flashing Damage Means More Than a Repair

Flashing and chimney repairs solve a lot of Dallas roof leaks. There's a point where they don't. If water has been getting in long enough that the roof decking around the chimney is soft, if the flashing failure is paired with a shingle field that's already brittle and losing granules, or if there are multiple failing transitions across an aging roof, patching one of them buys a few months at most.

In those cases the honest answer is a [roof replacement](/residential-roofing/roof-replacement), with new flashing, a proper cricket, and fresh underlayment integrated into the whole system from the start. We'll show you the moisture readings and the photos and let you make that call with real information. If a repair will genuinely hold, we'll tell you that too, because selling a replacement to someone who needs a $600 flashing fix isn't how we stay in business here.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How do I know if my Dallas roof leak is the flashing or the chimney itself?
Often you can't tell from inside, since water from a cracked chimney crown and water from failed flashing both show up as the same ceiling stain. An inspection that checks the crown, cap, mortar, and flashing together is the reliable way to find the actual entry point.

### Can flashing be repaired without replacing the whole roof?
Yes, in most cases. If the surrounding shingles are still flexible and the decking is solid, new step and counterflashing can be woven in as a standalone repair. Replacement becomes the answer when the leak has damaged the deck, or when the rest of the roof is near the end of its life.

### Why does my chimney leak during heavy or wind-driven rain but not a light shower?
That usually points to a marginal flashing detail or a missing cricket. In a light rain the water sheds fast enough that the weak spot holds. A hard, wind-driven Dallas storm pushes water sideways and uphill against that seam until it gets through.

### How long should a flashing repair last?
Counterflashing cut properly into the mortar joint should last for decades, well beyond the life of the current shingles. A caulk-based repair, by contrast, tends to fail within a few years in this climate, which is why the method matters more than the price.
