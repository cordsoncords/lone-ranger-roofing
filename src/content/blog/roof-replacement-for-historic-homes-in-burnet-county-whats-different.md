Burnet County has no shortage of homes that were standing long before the lake brought in the weekend crowds. Stone farmhouses outside town, old Victorian-style houses near the courthouse square, ranch homes with rooflines that have been patched and repatched for generations. A roof replacement on one of these houses is not the same project as re-roofing a ten-year-old build in a newer subdivision, and treating it that way is how historic homes end up with roofs that look wrong or perform worse than what they replaced.

We've worked on enough older Burnet County homes to know the differences show up before the first shingle comes off.

## The Decking and Framing Underneath Are Often the Real Story

Historic homes were often framed with full-dimension lumber and spaced decking rather than the plywood or OSB sheathing used in modern construction. Once the old roofing comes off, that framing needs to be evaluated on its own terms. Decades of patch jobs sometimes hid rot, undersized repairs, or framing that was never quite right to begin with.

A [roof inspection](/residential-roofing/roof-inspection) on a historic Burnet County home takes longer than a standard inspection for exactly this reason. We're checking the shingles and also what they've been sitting on for half a century or more.

## Matching the Roofline's Original Character

Many older Burnet County homes have rooflines, dormers, and trim details that modern tract construction doesn't bother with anymore. Steep pitches, exposed rafter tails, and decorative ridge details are common on homes built before mid-century construction standardized around simpler shapes.

Replacing the roof on one of these homes means working around those details rather than flattening them into something generic. A crew unfamiliar with older framing can accidentally simplify a roofline during replacement, and once that character is gone, it's gone for good.

## Material Choices That Respect the Home's Age

Standard three-tab or architectural shingles work fine on plenty of older homes, but some historic properties call for a different approach. [Metal roofing](/residential-roofing/metal-roofing) was common on older Texas farmhouses and ranch homes long before it became a modern trend, and on the right historic property, it's actually the more period-accurate choice, not a departure from it.

Color and profile matter here too. A bright, modern shingle color can clash with a home that's spent a century earning its weathered look. We talk through these choices with homeowners rather than defaulting to whatever is fastest to order.

## What to Expect From the Process on an Older Home

Historic home roof replacements in Burnet County often take a bit longer than a standard job because of what gets found once the old roofing comes off. Rotten decking, undersized rafters, or old repairs done with whatever materials were on hand at the time all need to be addressed properly rather than papered over. That can extend a one-day job to two, depending on what's underneath.

We document what we find and walk the homeowner through it before making repairs, so there are no surprises on the invoice and no corners cut on a home that's lasted this long for a reason.

## FAQ: Roof Replacement on Historic Burnet County Homes

**Does a historic home always need special roofing materials?**
Not always. Many older Burnet County homes do fine with standard asphalt shingles, though some benefit from metal roofing or specific profiles that match the home's original era.

**Why does an older home's roof replacement sometimes take longer?**
Decades of prior repairs and original framing choices often need to be addressed once the old roof comes off, which can add time compared to a standard modern re-roof.

**Will replacing the roof change how my historic home looks?**
It shouldn't, if the work is done with the original roofline and details in mind. We aim to preserve or improve the home's character, not simplify it.

**Can the decking underneath be reused on an older home?**
Sometimes, if it's sound. We evaluate it once the old roofing is off rather than assuming either way before we can see it.
