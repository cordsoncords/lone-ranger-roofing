## How a Killeen Storm Actually Damages a Roof

Hail does not damage a roof the way most people picture it. It rarely punches through shingles outright — it bruises them, knocking loose the granules that protect the asphalt underneath from UV light and weather. A bruised shingle can look fine from the ground and still be months away from cracking open in the next heavy rain. That is part of why a roof that "looks okay" after a storm can still be carrying real damage.

Wind works differently. Strong gusts get up under the shingle tabs and break the seal that keeps them flat against the roof deck. Once that seal is broken, water has a path underneath the shingle line, even if nothing looks visibly torn from the street. Combine a hailstorm with wind, which is the usual pattern in Central Texas, and a roof can end up with bruising in some sections and lifted seals in others, two different kinds of damage that need two different kinds of attention.

That combination is exactly why a proper [roof inspection](/residential-roofing/roof-inspection) matters more than a quick look from the driveway. We check the whole roof section by section, because damage from a Killeen storm rarely announces itself in one obvious spot.

## Repair or Replace: What Actually Decides It

### Age and Remaining Life

A roof that is five years into a twenty-five-year lifespan behaves differently under hail than one that is already at year eighteen. Younger shingles have more flexibility and more granule reserve, so isolated storm damage often repairs cleanly. Older roofs are usually closer to the point where storm damage is the tipping point rather than the cause — the storm did not create the problem so much as expose how much life was already gone.

### How Much of the Roof Is Affected

A repair makes sense when damage is contained to a section or two. Once bruising and lifted seals show up across most of the roof's surface, patching individual spots stops making financial sense, because the untouched sections are typically not far behind the damaged ones in wear. At that point a full [roof replacement](/residential-roofing/roof-replacement) protects the house better than a roof with three or four different repair patches on it.

### What the Decking Looks Like Underneath

Shingles get the attention, but the decking underneath tells the real story. If water has been getting in for a while, even a small area of soft or rotted decking changes the conversation from repair to replacement, because new shingles laid over compromised decking will not hold the way they should. We check this on every inspection rather than assuming the surface tells the whole story.

## FAQ

**Can hail damage a roof without leaving visible holes?**
Yes. Hail typically bruises shingles and strips granules rather than punching through, which is why damage often is not obvious from the ground or even from a quick walk of the yard.

**How long after a storm can I still file a claim in Killeen?**
Most Texas policies allow a window of a year or more, but insurers look more favorably on claims filed soon after the storm, since the damage is easier to tie directly to that specific event.

**Will a repair void my roof's warranty?**
Not on its own, but repairs done by a different contractor than the original installer, or repairs that do not match the existing materials, can create coverage gaps. That is worth asking about directly before any work starts.

**How do I know if my roof needs a full tear-off instead of a patch?**
The clearest signs are widespread damage rather than a single section, soft or rotted decking, and a roof that is already past the midpoint of its expected lifespan. A full inspection settles the question with more certainty than a visual check alone.
