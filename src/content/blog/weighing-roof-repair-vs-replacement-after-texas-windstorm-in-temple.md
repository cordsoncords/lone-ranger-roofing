The morning after a windstorm rolls through Temple, homeowners walk the yard counting shingles in the grass. A few pieces means a small problem, a bare patch on the slope means a big one. That's the instinct, and it's usually the wrong measure.

Wind damage that decides whether a Temple roof gets repaired or replaced is mostly invisible from the ground. When a gust gets under a shingle and lifts it, the adhesive seal strip that bonds it to the course below can break even if the shingle drops back down looking untouched. Do that across a whole slope and you have a roof full of shingles that are no longer sealed to the course beneath them, stripped of their wind rating, and waiting for the next storm to actually let go.

Here's how we sort a windstorm roof into repair or replacement in Temple, and why the count in the yard isn't the number that matters.

## What a Windstorm Actually Does to a Temple Roof

### The seal strip break

Every architectural shingle has a strip of factory adhesive that fuses it to the one below once the sun warms it. That bond is what gives a roof its wind rating. A strong windstorm flexes shingles upward and snaps that bond. The shingle can look perfect afterward and still be loose, because the adhesive doesn't re-bond once it's broken and contaminated with dust.

This is why a roof that lost six shingles in a storm might need far more than six replaced. The crew has to check seal integrity across every slope that faced the wind, not the visibly bare spot alone.

### Creased and lifted shingles

Shingles that lifted and folded back down often carry a hairline crease across the mat. That crease is a fracture line. Within a season or two the exposed part of the shingle breaks off along it, and the leak starts there. Creased shingles count as damaged even when they're still in place and shedding water for now.

### Damage at the edges and penetrations

Wind works hardest at the perimeter of the roof, the rakes and eaves, and around anything that sticks up. Lifted drip edge, torn flashing at a wall or chimney, and a spun or detached vent are common after a Temple windstorm and are frequent leak points if they're missed.

## When a Repair Is the Right Call

If the wind hit one slope, the seal strips on the other slopes check out, the shingles are still flexible, and the roof has years of life left in it, a [storm damage repair](/residential-roofing/storm-damage-repair) is the sound choice. Replacing the damaged section, resealing what's loose, and correcting the edge metal brings the roof back to where it was.

Repairs also make sense on newer roofs where matching shingles is realistic and the damage is contained. A well-scoped [roof repair](/residential-roofing/roof-repair) on a five-year-old Temple roof after a one-slope wind event is money well spent.

## When Replacement Is the Honest Answer

The math changes when the seal strips are broken across most of the roof, when the shingles are already brittle from age and UV, or when the roof has taken repeated storm hits and this is the latest. Patching a slope on a roof that's compromised everywhere else is spending good money on borrowed time.

An older roof that can't be color-matched is another case. A bright repair patch on a weathered roof isn't the real issue; the real issue is that the rest of the roof is close behind the damaged part. In those situations a [roof replacement](/residential-roofing/roof-replacement) resets the whole system, and most of ours finish in a single day. ## Frequently Asked Questions

### How do I know if wind broke the seal on shingles that look fine?
You generally can't from the ground or from a quick look. A roofer checks by lifting shingles by hand across the wind-facing slopes to feel whether the adhesive still holds. Widespread broken seals are a replacement signal even with few shingles missing.

### Does insurance cover wind damage to a roof in Temple?
Usually yes, when a specific windstorm caused it. Coverage turns on documenting the event and the full extent of the damage, including broken seals and creased shingles, not the missing pieces alone.

### My roof lost a few shingles and nothing else looks wrong. Why would I need a replacement?
Because the missing shingles may be the visible edge of a slope full of broken seal bonds. If the adhesive is gone across the roof, the whole thing has lost its wind rating even where it looks intact.

### How soon after a windstorm should I get the roof looked at?
Within a week or two. A creased or unsealed shingle still sheds water at first, so waiting for a leak means waiting for interior damage. An early inspection catches it while it's contained.
