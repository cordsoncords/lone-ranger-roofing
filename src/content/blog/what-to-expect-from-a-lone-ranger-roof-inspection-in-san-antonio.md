A lot of San Antonio homeowners put off calling for a roof inspection because they are not sure what the visit actually involves, or they assume it ends with someone pushing a replacement they do not need. That hesitation is fair, given how many storm-chasing crews have come through San Antonio after a hail event with a fast pitch and not much else behind it.

We get asked what to expect often enough that it is worth laying out plainly. Here is what actually happens from the first call to the final report, so there are no surprises when our truck pulls up.

## Before We Show Up

Scheduling starts with a short conversation about the roof's age, any known issues, and whether there has been recent storm activity in the area. This helps us know what to look for before we ever step on the roof, whether that is wind damage from a specific storm date or general wear tied to the roof's age.

We show up on time, walk the property first, and let the homeowner know what the visit will involve before climbing up. There is no pressure to sign anything before the inspection even starts, and no assumption made about what the roof needs until we have actually looked at it.

## What We Actually Check on the Roof

The inspection covers the full roof, not a partial glance from one side of the house. That means checking shingle or material condition across every section, examining flashing around chimneys, vents, and valleys, checking gutters and their attachment points, and looking for soft spots or signs of moisture in the deck underneath. San Antonio's mix of intense heat and sudden hailstorms wears on roofs differently than other parts of the state, so we pay close attention to UV wear on shingles alongside impact damage from hail.

We also check the attic when access allows, since signs of a leak often show up there well before they reach a ceiling. A stained rafter or damp insulation tells us something a homeowner might not notice from inside the house for months.

### Documenting What We Find

Every finding gets photographed, whether it is a section of roof in good shape or an area that needs attention. This documentation matters most when a homeowner is dealing with an insurance claim after storm damage, since photos tied to specific roof sections give an adjuster clear evidence to work from rather than a general description.

## What You Get After the Inspection

At the end of the visit, we walk the homeowner through what we found in plain terms, without jargon or manufactured urgency on issues that do not need it. If the roof is in good shape, we say so. If something needs attention, we explain what it is, why it matters, and what the options are, whether that is a [roof repair](/residential-roofing/roof-repair), a [storm damage repair](/residential-roofing/storm-damage-repair) tied to a specific weather event, or a full [roof replacement](/residential-roofing/roof-replacement) if the roof has reached the end of its service life.

A written report follows the visit, with photos and specifics a homeowner can keep on file, use for an insurance claim, or reference later when deciding on next steps. There is no obligation attached to that report. It belongs to the homeowner whether they choose to work with us on repairs or not.

## FAQ

**How long does a roof inspection in San Antonio typically take?**
Most inspections take between 30 and 45 minutes, depending on the size of the roof and whether attic access is available.

**Do I need to be home for the inspection?**
It helps, especially for the walkthrough afterward where we explain what was found, but it is not required if attic access is not needed.

**What if the inspection finds nothing wrong?**
We tell you exactly that. An inspection that always finds problems worth selling isn't giving an honest read of the roof's actual condition.

**Is there any cost or obligation for a Lone Ranger inspection?**
No. The inspection is free, and the report is yours to keep regardless of what you decide to do afterward.
